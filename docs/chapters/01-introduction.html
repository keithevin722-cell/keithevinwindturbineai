Chapter 1: Introduction to VAWTs

Vertical-axis wind turbines (VAWTs) spin around a vertical shaft, so they can accept wind from any direction without yaw controls. For hobbyists, that means fewer moving orientation parts and easier low-height experiments.

VAWT vs HAWT

  • VAWT: Good in turbulent or shifting winds, easier ground-level drivetrain access, lower tip heights.
  • HAWT: Usually higher efficiency in clean, steady wind and utility-scale systems.

Main VAWT types

  • Savonius: Drag-driven, high startup torque, lower peak efficiency, great for low-speed starts.
  • Darrieus (eggbeater): Lift-driven, better efficiency, often harder self-starting.
  • H-rotor: Straight-blade Darrieus variant, easier DIY fabrication and balancing.

Expected output and reality check

A small DIY unit in average suburban wind often produces modest daily energy. With a 1.5 m × 1.8 m rotor and a 24 V PMA, practical output may range from tens of watts in light winds to a few hundred watts in strong, steady winds.

Safety reminder: Blade failure, overspeed, and electrical faults are real risks. Never stand near spinning blades; use mechanical guards where practical; include braking/dump load strategies before first spin.
